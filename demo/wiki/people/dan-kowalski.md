## Overview

Dan Kowalski has taught modern European history at a public high school for eleven years. He is the Thursday Circle's historian-in-residence, which means he is frequently the one to say "this is not new" when the rest of the group believes they have identified a novel crisis. He is usually right, which is not always what the group wants to hear.

## The Teacher

Dan's classroom reputation has filtered back through his anecdotes: he is apparently the teacher students remember. He teaches the twentieth century as a study in how reasonable people, making reasonable decisions, produce catastrophic outcomes. His students find this disturbing. He finds their disturbance encouraging.

He has a Chekhov-like instinct for the telling detail — the thing that reveals character while pretending to describe furniture. When [[Yael Cohen]] asked him once how he stays motivated teaching the same material every year, he said: "Every year the Holocaust is new to someone." The table went quiet.

## Melancholy with Benefits

Dan's humor is the driest in the group. He delivers pronouncements about human nature with the flat affect of someone reading a weather report, which makes them funnier and more accurate. [[Priya Sharma]] once said Dan would be unbearable if he were wrong more often.

He reads slowly and rereads constantly. His recommendation of *Stoner* by John Williams has colonized the reading lives of everyone in the Thursday Circle. Each of them reported feeling vaguely accused by it. He considered this a success.
