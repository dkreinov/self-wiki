## Overview

Yael Cohen is a freelance illustrator whose clients range from children's books to technical documentation to editorial work. She joined the Thursday Circle through [[Alex Mercer]], who met her at a chess club exhibition — she was there to sketch the players, not play. She is the group's fastest thinker and most unpredictable conversationalist: she can be silent for forty minutes and then say the thing that reframes the entire evening.

## Sci-Fi as Philosophy

Yael reads science fiction as a form of philosophy — a way of isolating variables that reality keeps confounding. *The Left Hand of Darkness* by Ursula K. Le Guin is her most-cited book, a novel about gender and power she has re-read six times and finds differently useful each time. She gave [[Dan Kowalski]] a copy expecting resistance; he called her a week later and said it was the best book about history he had ever read, despite containing no history.

Her recommendation of *Invisible Cities* by Calvino to [[Alex Mercer]] opened a three-month period in which he brought it up constantly. She considered this a success.

## In the Group

Yael draws during conversations. She has sketchbooks full of the Thursday Circle — hands, expressions, table geometry. No one has seen these books in full. She occasionally shows a specific page to the specific person drawn on it, without comment.

Her assessments of people are rarely stated directly. They emerge from a question asked, or an observation so oblique it takes a moment to land. [[Priya Sharma]] once said that Yael's compliments arrive on a three-second delay.
