## Overview

Mr. Blonde has taught modern European history at a public high school for eleven years. He is The Reservoir's historian-in-residence, which means he is frequently the one to say "this is not new" when the rest of the group believes they have identified a novel crisis. He is usually right, which is not always what the group wants to hear.

## The Teacher

Mr. Blonde's classroom reputation has filtered back through his anecdotes: he is apparently the teacher students remember. He teaches the twentieth century as a study in how reasonable people, making reasonable decisions, produce catastrophic outcomes. His students find this disturbing. He finds their disturbance encouraging.

He has a Chekhov-like instinct for the telling detail — the thing that reveals character while pretending to describe furniture. When [[Mr. Silver]] asked him once how he stays motivated teaching the same material every year, he said: "Every year the Holocaust is new to someone." The table went quiet.

## The Drift

Mr. Blonde entered the group in 2015 describing himself as a moderate left-wing voter. By 2025 the group would struggle to apply that label. The change was not sudden — it accumulated through a decade of crisis, each one nudging his conclusions a little further from where they started. He does not acknowledge the drift directly. He says his positions have not changed; the world moved.

The group accepts this and does not accept it. His rage-quit cycles — leaving the chat, returning days later without comment — are most frequent after political arguments. [[Mr. Pink]] is the one who usually talks him back.

## Melancholy with Benefits

His humor is the driest in the group. He delivers pronouncements about human nature with the flat affect of someone reading a weather report, which makes them funnier and more accurate. [[Mr. Pink]] once said Mr. Blonde would be unbearable if he were wrong more often.

He reads slowly and rereads constantly. His recommendation of *Stoner* by John Williams has colonized the reading lives of everyone in The Reservoir. Each of them reported feeling vaguely accused by it. He considered this a success.
