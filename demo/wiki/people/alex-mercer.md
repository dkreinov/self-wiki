## Overview

Alex Mercer is a backend engineer who has been part of the Thursday Circle since its founding. He works on distributed systems during the day and reads philosophy at night — a combination the group finds both impressive and exhausting. His conversational style runs Socratic: he rarely states a position, preferring questions that destabilize everyone else's.

## Conversational Role

Alex is the Thursday Circle's designated complicator. When [[Priya Sharma]] says something with confident authority, Alex asks the one question that makes it less certain. When [[Dan Kowalski]] delivers a verdict on history, Alex finds the counterexample. He is not a contrarian — he agrees when he agrees — but his default mode is interrogation rather than assertion.

The group has a running joke: if Alex goes quiet for more than ten minutes, someone will say "uh oh" and the table will brace.

## Reading and Thinking

Alex reads philosophy of mind, mathematics, and computer science as a single continuous subject. His three most-pressed books — *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, *The Trial*, and *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance* — all circle the same question: what does it mean to think inside a system you cannot fully see? He presses them on others with the enthusiasm of someone who believes the answer is in there somewhere, if only someone would read carefully enough.

## Outside the Group

Alex cycles alone on weekends and plays chess at a club where he is reliably the youngest member by fifteen years. He says the old men have taught him more about patience than anything he has read.
