## Overview

Priya Sharma is an emergency physician and the Thursday Circle's most practically-minded member. She joined through [[Dan Kowalski]] during residency, when they discovered they had both grown up with the same edition of the same encyclopedia and both read it cover to cover. She has since become the group's anchor — the one who steers philosophical spirals back to something actionable.

## The Pragmatist

Priya's patience for theory is directly proportional to its proximity to something real. She follows [[Alex Mercer]]'s philosophical detours with interest up to the point where they detach from any human consequence, at which point she will say, flatly: "but what do you do with that?"

This is not anti-intellectualism. Priya reads more than anyone in the group and her recommendations are the ones most likely to change how you behave. She is simply impatient with abstraction that flatters the thinker without improving the world.

## Reading and Medicine

Her reading splits between medicine and history, which she treats as the same discipline approached from different directions. She gave [[Dan Kowalski]] a copy of *The Emperor of All Maladies* and watched him spend two weeks in a state of quiet devastation. She considers *Meditations* by Marcus Aurelius the most practical book written before the twentieth century. She lent her copy to [[Yael Cohen]] expecting it back in a week; Yael kept it three months and returned it with more annotations than Priya had made.

## In the Group

Priya is the first to arrive and the first to notice when someone at the table is not okay. She asks direct questions about things others dance around. The group trusts her assessments because they know she spent years in a place where assessment had to be right.
